1 · The problem

Modern KTX servers broadcast every hit into the demo (mvdhidden_dmgdone), which is what per-kill burst analysis (topKills) is built on. Roughly 45% of the demo archive predates that instrumentation — for those demos the damage stream is simply absent, and every burst-quality query is blind to them.

But old demos are not information-poor. They carry, per player and at server frame rate: health and armor over time (h/a), positions/view/velocity (pos), weapon-fire sounds (shots), LG beam segments, tracked rocket/grenade entity flights, item timelines, and the frag log. The engine reconstructs per-hit damage from health/armor decrements, attributes each hit from the spectator-visible evidence, and assembles the same burst rows the modern pipeline uses.

2 · Why it works — three structural facts

1. The h/a streams are per-hit, not sampled. svc_playerinfo is change-driven at server frame rate: 100% of ground-truth damage events have an exact-same-timestamp health or armor change, and frag times align with the killing h-drop (measured 129/129 on the calibration demo; frag-anchor rate across 85 old demos: p50 = 100%, p10 = 99.2%).

2. The observed delta IS the bounded damage. KTX-scoreboard "bounded" damage = armor absorbed + health share capped at remaining health — definitionally what the streams record. No damage model is needed for magnitude. And since bounded == raw for every surviving hit, magnitudes also discriminate weapons sharply: an LG cell is exactly 30, a rocket 100–120 − 0.5·dist.

3. Attribution has rich spectator-visible evidence. TE_LIGHTNING2 beam segments (exact frame; the victim lies on the segment), rocket/grenade entity flights (spawn + end, position + time), svc_sound weapon fire, full position/view/velocity tracks, and the frag log anchoring every kill. All verified present in the no_damage demos, and the damage physics of the old era are measured identical (LG = 30 with modern armor splits, splash D−0.5·dist, mega-rot present, no svc_playerinfo dropouts).

3 · The algorithm

1Delta extraction Per player, merge h+a change streams; drops = damage. Filter mega-rot, spawn resets, pickups. Health share capped at remaining health; corpse hits → 0.
2Attribution Score candidates per delta: geometry + damage-model consistency + knockback direction + enemy-prior. Best explanation wins; no candidate → environmental, excluded.
3Burst assembly killBurstFor parity: backward walk over killing-weapon hits, 3000 ms capture gap, life-start clip, anchor at the frag instant, returnDamage over 4000 ms.

Two discriminators sit on top of the geometry:

The damage-model score checks each candidate's expected magnitude against the observed delta — LG 30/cell (down to armor-only 9/18/24 for health-nullified server modes), splash D−0.5·dist for enemies / D−0.25·dist for self, pellet ranges, ×4 under quad (from q intervals). This is what disambiguates close-quarters rocket fights (whose explosion was it). Knockback adds a direction check: the victim's velocity change should point away from the candidate explosion.

The enemy-attribution prior (SELF_PEN): in the ambiguous close-range mutual-rocket frame, preferring the enemy explanation converts silent juicy-burst recall losses into rare slight inflations (duel juicy-RL recall 93.5%→98.7% for a 98.6%→96.2% precision trade, confirmed on a never-touched sample). Mode-aware default: 0.6 in duels, 0.1 in team games.

Burst-walk parity is proven by oracle: fed the real damage log, the Python walk reproduces -view top-kills 4398/4398 rows with zero mismatches on damage, hits, spanMs, maxGapMs and returnDamage.

5 · The first production run (2026-08-11)

41,766megademocache demos
27,267non-arena, no damage stream
26,242reconstructed (96.2%)
24,694games in out-recon · 2,449,226 kills

Wall-clock ≈ 21 min on 7 workers (~0.25 s heavy parse + ~0.03–1 s recon per demo). Exclusions, all logged per-sha: 1,025 demos with frags but no player streams (kill-level only — a known class, out of this tier), and 1,548 end-map games removed after the run on the ruling that end is arena. 2,577 games reconstructed to zero kills — spot-checked as genuinely fragless recordings (prewar/aborted).

Three input bugs found and fixed by the run

Non-finite coordinates crash the analyzer's JSON encoder (~1.1% of old demos — json: unsupported value: NaN). Root-caused by section-wise marshal probing to non-finite wire origins reaching four sinks: projectile flights, mover tracks (doors/plats — which also poisoned the derived floor-height column), item entities, and player position samples (→ derived velocities). Fixed with ingestion-side finite guards (4 files in mvd-analytics, +54/−10, currently uncommitted), built as a separate qw-analyze-viewall-nanfix binary; byte-identical output on unaffected demos.

The empty-wire-team duel bug (~4,000 duels — 15% of the old corpus — would have silently produced zero kills). Old duels often record an empty team string for both players; the analyzer's frag classifier then marks every frag isTeamKill, and the reconstructor skips teamkill frags. The streams block masks it (its team falls back to the player name). Fixed as input canonicalization in the driver: with exactly two players, a killer≠victim frag cannot be a teamkill. Recovered ~64,500 kills.

Null shots lists in three demos — normalized to empty so the engine sees "no evidence" instead of crashing.

6 · Trust model

Reconstructed damage is an estimate and must stay isolatable, never silently mixed with real damage:

markerwherevalues
dmgSourceevery kill row "reconstructed" here; real broadcast damage is "broadcast"
reconTierevery game row high 24,146 — Duel/Team, non-arena (the validated class)  ·  unknown-mode 429 — no mode in metadata  ·  other-mode 119 — FFA/Extinction/Wipeout/RACE, never validated  ·  excluded arena-family maps incl. end, CTF, no-streams demos

Rule of thumb from the evidence: selection survives better than per-burst value accuracy. Filters ("find contested RL bursts ≥ 180") transfer at 95–99% fidelity even where individual damage numbers wobble; treat single damage values as ±5% and rankings as solid.

9 · Quick start — one demo, end to end

# 1. Heavy parse: full streams + spatial evidence (~0.25 s, ~3–12 MB JSON)
MVDA_BSP_DIR=<dist>/bsps qw-analyze-viewall \
  -view full -include positions,view,velocity,projectiles,beams \
  demo.mvd > full.json

# 2. Reconstruct (prints event count + top-5 burst rows)
python3 recon.py full.json

As a library:

import recon
d      = recon.load("full.json")
events = recon.build_recon_events(d)            # per-hit damage events
rows   = recon.assemble_topkills(d, events)     # topKills-compatible burst rows
# rows[0] → {"killer": ..., "victim": ..., "time": ..., "weapon": "rl",
#            "damage": 300, "hits": 5, "spanMs": 2936, "maxGapMs": 843,
#            "victimWep": "rl", "returnDamage": 78}

Sanity check for a first run: on a typical duel you should see roughly one burst row per enemy frag, and nevents a few× the frag count. A duel yielding zero rows almost always means the empty-wire-team gotcha (§13).

11 · Validate on your own demos before trusting anything

The engine's leverage is that modern demos carry both the real damage broadcast and the h/a/pos streams — so you can reconstruct blind and score against truth on any corpus you care about:

python3 freshval_prep.py myval 4242 60    # 60 modern demos, YOUR unused seed
python3 holdout_eval.py myval

Expect ≈ 90% exact / ≈ 95% within-5% / Spearman ≈ 0.97+ on non-arena duel/team. The bar that matters is rank preservation for your archetypes (the precision/recall block), not absolute damage error. If your corpus skews to arena-family maps or CTF, expect the documented degradation and tier accordingly.

13 · Input contract & gotchas

recon.py reads one JSON per demo with: streams.players[] (per player: h/a change streams, pos track, sp spawn times, alive intervals, weapon-held intervals rl/lg/gl/ssg/sng/ng, quad intervals q, team), streams.beams, streams.projectiles, frags.frags, shots.shots, and optional demoInfo.mode. The extraction command in §9 produces exactly this.

Gotchas, in the order they will bite you:

1 · Empty-wire-team duels (~15% of old duels). Both players record an empty team string → every frag is flagged isTeamKill → recon skips them all → zero rows, silently. Canonicalize before calling the engine (this is what recon_run.py does): in a 2-player demo, a killer≠victim frag is not a teamkill.

2 · The analyzer NaN crash (~1% of old demos). Non-finite wire origins abort the JSON encode (json: unsupported value: NaN). The fix is four small ingestion guards in mvd-analytics (drop non-finite samples in flightsToStream, mover-track append, recordPosition; zero non-finite item origins) — currently an uncommitted working-tree patch built as qw-analyze-viewall-nanfix. Without it, catch the nonzero exit and count the demo out.

3 · No player streams (~4% of old demos). Frag log exists, h/a/pos don't — the extraction has no streams block and reconstruction is impossible. Kill-level data only; exclude from this tier.

4 · demoInfo.mode is usually absent in old demos. The engine falls back to player count for the SELF_PEN mode split (2 players → duel). Fine in practice; be aware if you feed it exotic formats.

5 · Null shots/frags inner lists occur in a handful of demos — normalize to [].

15 · Known limits (all measured, none hidden)

· Same-frame multi-hit merges cannot be split — ~1–2% of rows off-by-a-hit; damage unaffected.

· Close-quarters rocket self/enemy ambiguity is the dominant residual error class; knockback + aim + consumed-shot logic + the enemy prior already reclaim most of it.

· returnDamage within-5%: 92.8% — it inherits attribution symmetrically.

· RAW-family (overkill) damage is only recoverable to the −99 corpse clamp; this pipeline uses bounded, so it does not matter here.

· Arena-family maps (povdmm4/dmm4*/anarena/end/…) run ~83% within-5% — excluded from the corpus. CTF is unmodeled (hook/rail) — excluded. boundedMode:skipped modes (midair, instagib): ground truth itself refuses these; skip reconstruction too.

· The cadence test quantizes times but keeps modern-precision projectile end positions, so its 92.6% is mildly optimistic on that one axis; the old-corpus internal evidence (98% of damage attributed, matching physics distributions) covers the real condition.
